privacy policy

Your climbing stays on your iPhone, unless you share it with friends.

Effective 17 November 2026

In short: Summit has no account of its own, no server of ours, no advertising and no analytics. It reads the flights of stairs you climb from Apple Health on your iPhone and works everything out on your iPhone. Nothing leaves your iPhone unless you choose to climb with friends (Summit 1.1 and later). If you do, a first name you type, the climb you are on, how high you are on it and, for a climb you climb together, when you set off and reached the top are kept in Apple's iCloud so that the copies of Summit of the people you climb with can show them. Nothing is ever sold or used for advertising.

This policy explains what the Summit app for iPhone (“Summit”, “we”, “us”) does with information on your device. If anything here is unclear, email joshuajameshell@gmail.com.

What Summit reads, and why

Flights Climbed, from Apple Health

With your permission, Summit reads one type of data from Apple Health: Flights Climbed, the floors your iPhone or Apple Watch has counted. Summit uses it to total your climbing, move you up the peak you have chosen, keep your streak and show your history. Summit only reads this data. It never writes to Apple Health.

Summit's home-screen and lock-screen widgets read the same Flights Climbed data, on your iPhone, so they can show your progress between visits to the app.

Motion, from your iPhone

With your permission, Summit reads the floors your iPhone's motion sensors count while the app is open, to show your climb as it happens. This is used for the live display only and is not stored.

Where your data is kept

Everything Summit works out (your daily totals, your expeditions, your settings and the pictures for the widgets) is stored on your iPhone, in Summit's own storage. None of it is sent to us. We do not run a server and Summit has no account to sign in to. The one exception is climbing with friends, described next, which is off until you turn it on.

Like any app's storage, it may be included in your iPhone's own backups (to iCloud or a computer), which Apple or your computer keep under your control. Your Apple Health data is kept and backed up by Apple Health itself.

Climbing with friends (optional)

From version 1.1 you can add friends by sending them a link, and from version 1.2 you can invite them to a climb, set off together as a group and see one another on it. This is off until you open Profile, then Friends, and type a first name. Until then nothing described in this section happens. It needs your iPhone to be signed in to iCloud.

What is stored

  • The first name you type (up to 24 characters). It does not have to be your real name.
  • Your climb: which climb you are on, how many metres up it you are, whether it is paused, when you started it, when you reached the summit, and when this was last updated.
  • An identifier that Apple's iCloud makes for you for Summit alone. It is not your Apple Account, your email address or your phone number, and it does not reveal them.
  • Your friendships: a record for each friend you add or remove, holding the two identifiers.
  • Invitations: when you invite a friend to a climb, the climb, your first name, the two identifiers, the group the invitation is to and when it was sent.
  • Climbs together (version 1.2 and later): for each climb you climb in a group, a record holding the group (a random code made for that climb), the climb, your first name, your identifier, when you set off, how many metres up you are, whether you have paused or left, when you reached the top, and when this was last updated. It is kept after you move on to another climb, so that the order everyone finished in, and the trophies that go with it, stay as they were.

Nothing else is stored. Your Flights Climbed, your daily totals, your lifetime total, your streak and your purchases never leave your iPhone. Summit never reads your location or your contacts. The height on your climb is worked out from Flights Climbed, and it is the only figure from Apple Health that is shared.

Where it is stored, and who can read it

It is kept in Apple's iCloud (CloudKit), in the shared database of Summit's iCloud container, which Apple runs. See Apple's privacy policy. We still do not run a server of our own.

Summit shows what you share only to the friends you have added and to the people you climb in a group with, and only you can change or remove your own records. Everyone in a group sees everyone else in it (their first name, their place on the climb and their time to the top), including someone a friend invited whom you have not added yourself. But please understand how the store works: it is one shared database that every copy of Summit can read, not a private space for you and your friends, and it is not end-to-end encrypted. Someone with the technical means could read the first name, climb and height held against an identifier without being your friend, and we can read it through Apple's developer tools, which we do only to fix a problem. So do not type anything as your name that you would not want seen.

Your invite link contains your identifier and your first name and, when it invites someone to a climb, that climb and its group. Anyone who opens it in Summit becomes your friend, until you remove them.

What it is used for

Only to show you and the people you climb with to one another: on the climb, in the standings, in the results and trophies of a climb finished together and in the Friends list, and to tell you on your iPhone when a friend invites you, adds you, says yes, passes you or reaches the top. It is never used for advertising or marketing, never sold, and never passed to anyone else.

How long it is kept, and how to remove it

  • Stop sharing with friends, at the foot of the Friends screen, deletes your first name, your climb and your height from iCloud, along with your records of climbs together, removes your friends and withdraws your invitations. What stays is a record for each friend you removed, holding the two identifiers and nothing else, so that an old link cannot quietly reconnect you.
  • Remove friend, in a friend's menu, stops the two of you seeing each other.
  • An invitation stops being shown after 14 days and is deleted the next time the phone that sent it checks. You can also cancel one from the climb, which deletes it.
  • Your record of a climb together stays until you stop sharing, as described above. Leaving such a climb marks you as having left it, so the others' results no longer wait for you.
  • Deleting Summit does not delete what is in iCloud. Stop sharing first. If you have already deleted the app, install it again and stop sharing, or email us with the first name you used and the climb you were on and we will find and delete it.

Opening an invitation without Summit (the App Clip)

If someone opens a friend's invitation on an iPhone that does not have Summit, iOS may show a small part of Summit called an App Clip. It reads the link it was opened with (the friend's identifier and first name, the climb and its group) to show the invitation. It keeps that invitation on the iPhone, for up to 30 days, so that Summit can pick it up once it is installed. The App Clip does not read Apple Health, has no purchases and sends nothing to iCloud, to us or to anyone else.

The invitation's web page does something similar when you tap Get Summit: it copies the invitation's link to your clipboard, and Summit reads it only if you then tap Paste on its first screen.

Health data

Health data Summit reads from Apple Health is never used for advertising or marketing, never sold, and never used for any purpose other than showing your climbing in Summit. It is not shared with anyone, with one exception that you choose: if you climb with friends, the height you have reached on your current climb is shown to them, as described above.

Purchases

Summit Pro is sold through the App Store. Apple handles the payment and your payment details; Summit only learns, on your iPhone, whether you have an active subscription so it can unlock Summit Pro. See Apple's privacy policy.

Notifications

Summit's notifications (your morning briefing, a reminder before a free trial ends, a summit when you reach one, news of your friends) are worked out and scheduled on your iPhone. You can turn the daily ones off in Summit's settings, or all of them in iOS Settings. If you climb with friends, iCloud may send your iPhone a silent signal through Apple's push service when a friend invites you, so that Summit can check for it; Apple handles the delivery.

Sharing

When you choose to share a summit, Summit makes an image of your climb on your iPhone and opens the iOS share sheet. Nothing is shared unless you choose where to send it.

Ratings and crash reports

Summit may show Apple's own prompt asking you to rate the app; your rating goes to the App Store. If you have chosen in iOS Settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may send us anonymous crash reports and usage statistics. We use these only to fix problems.

Tracking and third parties

Summit does not track you across apps or websites, contains no advertising, and includes no third-party analytics or advertising code.

Children

Summit is not directed at children. Unless someone chooses to climb with friends, Summit collects no personal information from anyone. Climbing with friends needs an iCloud account, and should be set up by a child only with a parent's or guardian's agreement.

Your choices

  • You can stop Summit reading Flights Climbed at any time: open the Health app, tap your profile picture, then Apps, then Summit.
  • You can turn off motion access in Settings > Privacy & Security > Motion & Fitness.
  • You can stop climbing with friends at any time: Profile, Friends, Stop sharing with friends.
  • Deleting Summit removes everything it stored on your iPhone. Your Apple Health data is not affected. If you climb with friends, stop sharing before you delete the app (see above).

If you have never climbed with friends, we hold no data about you, and there is nothing for us to access, correct or delete on our side. If you have, you can see, change and delete what is stored from the Friends screen, and you can ask us to do it for you, or ask anything else about your data, by email at any time. If you are in the UK or the EU you also have the right to complain to your data protection authority (in the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office).

Changes to this policy

If Summit's handling of data changes, this page will be updated before the change is released, and its effective date changed. 15 November 2026: added climbing with friends, ahead of Summit 1.1. Before that, nothing left your iPhone. 17 November 2026: added climbing together as a group (what is kept, who sees it and how long for) and the App Clip, ahead of Summit 1.2.
