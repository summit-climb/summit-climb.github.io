Summit shows no climbing

Summit counts the Flights Climbed your iPhone (and Apple Watch, if you wear one) records in Apple Health. If Summit shows nothing:

  1. Open the Health app, tap your profile picture, then Apps, then Summit, and make sure Flights Climbed is turned on.
  2. Check that your iPhone is counting flights: in the Health app, search for Flights Climbed. If it is empty, check Settings > Privacy & Security > Motion & Fitness and make sure Fitness Tracking is on.
  3. Carry your iPhone as you climb. One flight is about 3 metres (10 feet) of climbing, so a gentle slope or a lift does not count.

My climbing is behind

Apple Health can take a few minutes to pass on new flights, and iOS decides how often Summit is woken in the background. Opening Summit always brings it up to date.

The widget is not up to date

The widget reads your flights from Apple Health while your iPhone is unlocked, as often as iOS allows (usually every 15 to 60 minutes). Apple Health cannot be read while your iPhone is locked, so the widget catches up after you unlock it. Opening Summit updates the widget straight away and redraws its picture.

Summit Pro and the free week

  • Free week: when you start Summit Pro's free week, Summit reminds you two days before it ends (if notifications are allowed). Cancel at least 24 hours before the week ends and you will not be charged.
  • Cancel or change your plan: open Settings, tap your name, then Subscriptions, then Summit. You can also use Manage subscription in Summit's settings.
  • New phone or reinstalled: open Summit's settings and tap Restore purchases, signed in with the same Apple Account you subscribed with.
  • Refunds: payments go through Apple, so refunds are requested from Apple at reportaproblem.apple.com.

If Summit Pro lapses part way up a Summit Pro peak, the climb pauses with your progress kept, and carries on when Summit Pro is back.

Notifications

You can choose which notifications Summit sends, and the time of the morning briefing, in Summit's settings. To turn them all off, open Settings > Notifications > Summit.

Your data

Summit has no account and keeps everything on your iPhone, unless you choose to climb with friends. Deleting the app removes your expeditions and settings; if you install it again, your climbing history is read back from Apple Health. If you climb with friends, tap Stop sharing with friends at the foot of the Friends screen before deleting the app, which removes your name and climb from iCloud. See the privacy policy.
